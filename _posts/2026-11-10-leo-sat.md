## Key Idea
The authors propose and emperically validate that current Congestion Control Algorithms (CCAs) designed for conventional terrestrial networks fail in Low-Earth Orbit (LEO) networks due to stochastic channel dynamics. They assert that the fundamental assumptions of CCAs are broken in LEO networks, as validated by measurements using 10 common CCAs on Starlink networks. This motivates their introduction of LeoCC. LeoCC takes advantage of detectable Reconfiguration Intervals (RI), which they can use to calibrate the algorithm to when the link is in a reconfiguring state. When compared to traditional CCAs they find that their algorithm can achieve the best throughput and latency balance given the dynamic network conditions.

## Critique
LeoCC relies heavily on artifacts that are specific to Starlink. Most significantly, the response interval threshold of 45ms. This magic number may no longer be accurate during changes to the Starlink system, or across other LEO systems.

## Connections
This relates to Raha and QUIC. Raha is a method to calcualte WAN degredation. Both LeoCC and Raha take similar approches at prediction of network degredation.
