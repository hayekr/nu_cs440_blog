## Key Idea
The authors propose OpenFLAME, a federated mapping infrastructure that allows for simple distribution of heterogenous maps. They implement this using separate map servers and clients using DNS style infrastructure to discover and use the maps. Map boundaries are defined using S2 cells to map spatial information to DNS geo-domains.

## Critique
While this infrastructure is an interesting repurposing of DNS, the foundational problem presented by the authors is manufactured. The authors argue that decentralized-federated mapping systems are needed to accommodate for a system that includes indoor and/or private maps. However, institutions currently solve this using proprietary maps, and they do not actively lobby for federated discovery. Additionally, indoor maps that the public finds great use for, e.g., airports and shopping centers, are already well served by centralized or crowdsourced platforms. As a result, OpenFLAME requires substantial infrastructure development and coordination overhead that severely limits the feasibility of this system.
