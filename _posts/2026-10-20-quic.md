## Key Idea
The authors evaluate five datasets of QUIC background traffic from the UCSD network telescope from 2021-2025. In their analysis the extract information from unencrypted QUIC packet headers from two vantage points, and remove known false positives and background scanning traffic. For off-net deployments the authors establish an active method of measurement by creating QUIC connections. The authors acknowledge limitations in determining what of collected traffic is due to back scatter, flow records are limited in history, and that encryption limits observations. The key contribution is a new method to passively measure hypergiant QUIC deployments and reveal stack configurations based on the limited information that QUIC exposes. Their methodology is validated using active measurement probing of multiple ASes. This active probing was executed to create a ground-truth confirmation of off-net deployments and evaluate the robustness of the passive measurements.

## Critique
While the article is well written and provides valuable insights into hypergiant QUIC infrastructure, it suffers from an inherent visibility bias. Measurements relying on Internet Background Radiation (IBR) depend entirely on unsolicited backscatter and indiscriminate scanning. Because deployments are only visible when triggered by spoofed traffic or reflection attacks, the volume of observed traffic is highly uneven. Consequently, the findings may not generalize to edge nodes, newer clusters, or well-filtered deployments that rarely appear in darknet traces.
